# Native-language SEO: first content batch

Research and editorial review: 16 September 2026. Initial markets: Germany, France, Spain and the Netherlands. Language URLs remain country-neutral so useful articles can also reach readers elsewhere.

## What this research establishes

Live searches were used to identify native wording, the kinds of pages returned, competing content and fit with Budgero. **Monthly search volume and keyword difficulty are unverified.** No Keyword Planner, Search Console or paid keyword database was connected. The local advertising export is an older English campaign performance report, not market-wide or native-language keyword-volume data.

The priorities below are editorial judgments about audience, intent and product fit. They are not a measured volume ranking. Result counts, social views, ads impressions and the number of competitors must not be substituted for monthly searches.

Start with one original article per non-English language and one English Actual Budget landing page. Keep related synonyms on the same useful page. Existing YNAB comparison pages already cover the switching intent; the new articles link to them rather than creating another generic comparison for every phrase.

### Supporting method terms

- German: **Zero-Base-Budgeting**, **nullbasierte Budgetierung**, **Umschlagmethode**.
- French: **budget base zéro**, **enveloppes budgétaires**, **gérer son budget**.
- Spanish: **presupuesto base cero personal**, **presupuesto personal**, **presupuesto familiar**.
- Dutch: **zero-based budgetteren**, **budgetteren met potjes**, **maandbudget maken**.

Unqualified zero-based-budgeting queries can have business or public-finance intent, especially in Spanish and Dutch. Household examples and qualified phrases are a closer match to this product. The French envelope/card-payment story offers a practical consumer angle beyond the existing method page and product manual.

Native Actual phrases to test later are **Alternative zu Actual Budget**, **alternative à Actual Budget**, **alternativa a Actual Budget**, and **alternatief voor Actual Budget**. Observed native results were sparse and often mixed with English. Treat this as a focused product-fit hypothesis, not evidence of high search volume. Identify the product as Actual Budget from actualbudget.org to avoid name ambiguity.

### Actual Budget and comparison accuracy

The new page uses official documentation: [installation](https://actualbudget.org/docs/install/), [currency limitations](https://actualbudget.org/docs/budgeting/multi-currency/), [encrypted sync](https://actualbudget.org/docs/getting-started/sync/), [bank integrations](https://actualbudget.org/docs/advanced/bank-sync/) and [account exports](https://actualbudget.org/docs/tour/accounts/). [YNAB’s official pricing and FAQ](https://www.ynab.com/pricing) support its bank-coverage qualification. Budgero claims are checked against its current product documentation.

Avoid recurring comparison errors: Actual does not universally require Docker; third-party hosting exists; its encryption is optional; it offers supported bank integrations. Budgero has no built-in bank connection and no one-click Actual budget-file importer. Sample screenshots demonstrate Budgero, not hands-on testing of competitors.

## Technical foundations included

- Original posts live in `content/blog/<locale>/<native-slug>.mdx`. Each has a self-canonical URL, language-specific listing and `Article.inLanguage`.
- Independent articles have no invented `hreflang` counterparts. Shared translated pages and documentation keep their real language alternates.
- Previously translated shells of English articles redirect to the English canonical. Unrelated foreign-language article paths return 404.
- Explicit URLs determine language. Automatic language redirects and automatic middleware alternate-link headers are disabled.
- Sitemap guide entries use actual localized documents once, fixing duplicated locale prefixes. The English RSS feed includes only published English articles.
- Language switching from an original article opens the destination language’s blog index.

This follows [Google’s multilingual-site guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites) and [alternate-version guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions). See also [next-intl routing configuration](https://next-intl.dev/docs/routing/configuration).

## What to measure next

1. Validate the exact shortlist in Keyword Planner using each country/language and the same trailing 12-month period. Record source, date, location, match/grouping behavior and whether volume is a range. Until then, leave volume blank.
2. After deployment, verify live status, canonicals, robots, sitemap membership and internal links. Check Search Console’s selected canonical and indexing for the five new URLs.
3. Compare completed 28-day windows after indexing: impressions, clicks, click-through rate and country/query mix per page. Keep branded and non-branded queries separate. Do not set a ranking deadline or infer demand from a page that is not indexed.
4. Track trial starts separately from organic visibility, using the project’s existing consent and analytics behavior. A broad educational query and an Actual-switching query have different jobs.
5. Expand only after measured queries identify the next useful topic. Check the Actual landing page against the existing comparison article, and the new guides against current YNAB/method pages, for overlapping queries.

## Local verification

- Production website build, standalone TypeScript check, changed-file ESLint and formatting passed.
- Nine focused content-routing tests passed (`pnpm --dir packages/website test:content`).
- All 244 unique sitemap URLs returned direct 200 responses, with self-canonicals, no `noindex`, and valid reciprocal language alternates where applicable. No duplicate language prefixes remained.
- All four native article URLs had the correct HTML language and `Article.inLanguage`, no invented translations, and membership in the correct blog listing. The RSS feed stayed English-only.
- Sixteen wrong-language article requests returned 404. Eight legacy-English/Actual requests redirected permanently without loops, including requests with a German language cookie and preference header.
- All five new pages fit at 320px, 390px and 1440px. Tables and localized screenshots rendered, and article language switching opened the destination blog index.

No campaign spending, publication, deployment or Search Console submission is part of this local content batch. Local checks do not establish live indexing or search demand.
